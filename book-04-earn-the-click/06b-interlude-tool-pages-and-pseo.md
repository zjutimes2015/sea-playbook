# Interlude — Tool Pages, Comparisons, and the Factory Temptation

This interlude exists so Book 4 does not pretend every site is a blog, and so handbook 12 does not sneak out of the series as a sixth title.

If you ship an AI tool, a template library, a converter, or a site whose product *is* a page that does a job, your search unit is often not an article. It is a **tool page**, a **template or scene page**, a **comparison or alternative page**, or a **directory-like index** that helps people discover a thing. Those page types have rules. They also have a famous failure mode: programmatic SEO as a factory that produces ten thousand nearly identical URLs and calls the sameness “coverage.”

Thursday Packet is already a tool. The builder’s temptation, after the first keyword card, was to generate a page for every file type and every typographic feature: `/convert/docx`, `/convert/idml`, `/convert/markdown`, `/ligatures`, `/small-caps`, and a hundred more, each with a unique title and a paragraph a model could spin. That is a factory with typography clothing.

I want you to use this bench before Chapter 7. Links will not save a factory. Citations will not save it either.

---

## Start With Who Is Searching, Not With a Root Word

A tool site has at least three searchers, and they do not want the same document.

**The person who will use the tool today.** They want the job done: a preview, a default that is sane, an output they can trust. SEO modules come after the job, or they interfere with it.

**The person who will pay.** They want limits, price, privacy, and a reason this is not a free generic. A pricing or compare page may be their document. A lyrical blog post usually is not.

**The person who is learning.** They want a worked example, a glossary you actually use, or a guide that passes the last-click test. They may become a user. They may not. Do not pretend every learner is a buyer, and do not pretend you can skip them if they are the only people asking public questions.

The SERP still decides the container. If the results for a query are all tool pages, an essay is swimming upstream. If they are all “X vs Y” tables, a feature list on the homepage may never be chosen. If they are all official docs, you may be in Chapter 3’s 0-score bucket.

## Page Types That Usually Earn Their Keep

**Tool pages.** The first screen does the task, or starts it honestly. The explanation, the limits, the related tools, and the structured data sit around a working surface, not instead of it. Thursday Packet’s `/repair` is a tool page when the preview is real. If the first screen is a thousand words about the future of typography and the tool is below a fold of theater, you wrote an article with a widget.

**Template or scene pages.** A specific job, a specific starting file, a specific output. “Thursday client agenda with name repair already applied” is a scene. “Templates” as a junk drawer is not. Each scene needs an increment — a reason this starting point is not a clone of the last one with three words swapped.

**Comparisons and alternatives.** These pages exist because people type them. They go badly when they are propaganda. A comparison that can be lifted should name what you do not do, who should pick the other tool, and which job is actually in common. If you cannot be fair in a sentence, do not publish the URL. Answer engines are especially unkind to syrupy “X is the best” pages, and so are people.

**Guides and lists**, when they are not moss. A list of “best typography exporters” that you quietly win is an old trick and a trust tax. A list of failure modes you tested, with dates, is an increment.

**Directories and navigation pages.** A honest index can help discovery — internally, and sometimes off-site, when a directory is a real map of a category. It has a double use: people navigate, and systems learn what exists. The boundary with Chapter 7 is sharp. A directory that exists to drop a link farm into your footer is not a map. It is a scheme with a nicer name. If you submit to directories, submit where a human would still browse, and fill in who you are, what you replace, and what job you do. A raw URL in a queue is not a mention.

## Programmatic Pages Have Two Edges

There is a legitimate version of scale.

You have a structured inventory that is **actually different in the job**: cities with different rules, templates with different fields, products with different constraints, languages with different typographic behavior. Each URL has unique inputs, unique outputs, and a unique limitation. The template is a template. The facts are not.

There is an illegitimate version that search systems have been cleaning up for years, under various names. Ten thousand URLs, thin text, swapped keywords, no reason for a human to prefer one to another. A sitemap that looks like success and a coverage report that looks like a cemetery. Answer engines, if they look at all, have even less reason to lift a sentence that is plainly a template slot.

If you cannot describe the increment of URL #847 without reading the template, you are building the second version.

Internationalization is a cousin of this problem. Translated shells without translated facts are a factory in another language. If the market is real, do the adult work: a native reader, a consistent language version, and official hreflang guidance — not a nightly machine pass over the attic.

## A Worked Page-Type Card (Composite)

| Proposed URL | Searcher | Container in the results | Increment in one sentence | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `/repair` | User and buyer | Mixed: thin posts, no honest preview | The preview shows repaired names before payment, and names three failing scripts | Keep; this is the unit |
| `/convert/markdown` | User | Generic converters | None we can say without reading the `/repair` template | Do not ship |
| `/templates/thursday-agenda` | User | Almost empty | A starting file whose name fields already survive the export | Later pile, one scene only |
| `/compare/generic-converters` | Buyer | Almost empty | A fair table that says who should still use a generic tool | Later pile, after last-click holds |
| `/blog/what-is-typography` | Learner | Encyclopedias | None | Never |

The card is a brake. It is also a kindness to the builder who wanted a hundred file-type URLs to feel busy. Busyness is not a page type.

## A Ninety-Minute Bench (When the Factory Is Calling)

1. List the page types you actually need this month: tool, scene, comparison, guide. No others.  
2. For each proposed URL, write the increment in one sentence and the searcher (user, buyer, learner).  
3. Open the current results and name the container.  
4. If more than three proposed URLs share a template, write down what is genuinely unique on each. If you cannot, collapse them.  
5. Make sure the tool works before the copy is polished.  
6. Only then touch titles, internal links, and schema that match the visible page.

If step 4 takes more than the rest of the hour, you were about to build a factory. That is a successful bench.

---

## Where This Interlude Falsifies Itself

**A media site or a marketplace may need scale that looks programmatic** and still be honest, if each URL is a real entity — a true listing, a true record. The honesty test remains: would a person choose this URL over the sibling?

**One comparison page can be enough for a year.** Do not build a matrix of “Thursday Packet vs X” for every competitor you privately fear. Fear is not demand.

**Handbooks and official documentation sometimes outrank every tool page**, and they should. Your interlude job then is to be the place the job can be finished, not the place the term is defined.

**This interlude is not Book 6.** If the series later opens a vertical volume on search-fit AI tools, it will be a queue decision. Until then, steal the page types and leave the rest.

---

## Takeaway

**Rule:** A tool site earns a URL when the page does a job a sibling page cannot do. Scale without that difference is a factory.

**This week:** Cancel any draft URL whose increment you cannot say without reading a template. Put the energy into the one tool page that already has a last-click.
