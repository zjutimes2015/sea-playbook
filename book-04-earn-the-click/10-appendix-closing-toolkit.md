## G. How This Appendix Relates to Book 5

If your verdict is **keep** or **deepen**, carry forward: the owner URL, the increment sentence, the event names (especially first touch and purchase), the later pile, the probe questions, and the hole list. Book 5 — *Numbers That Decide* — starts at breakpoints, stop and scale rules, and vetoes a one-person company can actually use. It does not reopen a factory because a chart felt lonely, and it does not treat a coverage badge as a finished Thursday.

If your verdict is **prune** or **diagnose first**, this appendix’s value is operational and emotional: you have artifacts that prove you worked like an adult. That dignity is the product of Book 4 even when the query report is quiet.

## H. Explicit Perimeter (Again)

Filled examples use invented `example.invalid` language and composite Thursday Packet scenes. They are **not** reports of a real company’s index rate, rank, click-through rate, citation share, referring domains, or payout.

Handbook figures cited elsewhere in the book — an index-rate movement from about 17% toward nearly 100%, an organic-traffic shape of about +40%, a second-hand editing experiment of about 68 sites and 744 articles, a “about four times the traffic” contrast, “ten times the traffic” title language, programmatic-SEO visit narratives, and any paid-click or recall figures carried forward from Books 1–3 — remain teaching cases only.

No ticked card guarantees indexing, ranking, citations, or income, and no composite fortnight should be quoted as if it happened to a named company. A complete toolkit only raises the odds you will refuse a page factory, refuse a coverage ceremony, and refuse to buy a count, before any of those becomes your week.
