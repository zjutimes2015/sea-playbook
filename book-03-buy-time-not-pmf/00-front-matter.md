# Buy Time, Not PMF

**Subtitle:** How to Own One or Two Channels and Use Ads as a Clock, Not a Verdict

**Series:** The Sea Playbook — Book 3 of 5

**Author:** Micheal (An Yaming)

**Edition note:** English Kindle manuscript for manual Amazon KDP upload. No auto-publish bots. Handbook case figures elsewhere are teaching shapes, not forecasts, traffic guarantees, or proof of product-market fit.

---

## Promise

After you finish, you should be able to turn a Book 2 ship verdict into **a written channel plan**: one primary channel, at most one auxiliary, a way every visit can settle into something you own, and — if you spend money — a clock that buys feedback rather than a ceremony that blesses a click-through rate.

You should also leave with a **tracking-acceptance card** and a dated keep / switch / pause / fix-the-journey verdict, written before anyone is allowed to call attention a market.

That is a skill promise: artifacts, gates, and a clock. It is not a traffic promise, and it is not a revenue promise.

## Not a Promise

Nothing in this book guarantees visits, ranking, follower counts, click-through rates, conversion, approval, payout, or a revenue tier.

Handbook teaching cases you will meet — a Google Ads stretch of about **2,159 HKD** spent against about **2,823 HKD** in attributed revenue (API cost excluded), a Reddit Ads run with a lively click-through rate and **zero** paid orders, an email recall of **190** messages that brought **eight** people back, a Chinese-language account story that grew into the low hundreds of followers, a hot-reply experiment with a large impression spike, a Black Friday week-board figure, programmatic-SEO “millions of visits” narratives, AdSense path sketches and RPM ranges — are **illustrations of shape**. Study the shape. Do not tattoo the numbers on your OKRs. Do not quote them as what *your* week will spend, earn, or prove.

If a sentence ever sounds like certainty for sale, reread this page. A click-through rate in a screenshot is the cathedral’s favorite new costume.

## The Rule You Can Say Out Loud

Own one or two channels.  
Ads buy time, not fit.  
A click-through rate is not a market.

Keep the three layers separate. A rented platform can lend you a crowd. A paid campaign can shorten the wait for a signal. An owned list or a product hook can still reach someone when the algorithm changes its mind. None of those layers, by itself, is a verdict on the job you claimed to do.

Book 1’s rule still stands behind this one: inspiration is a seed, a complaint is evidence, a payment is a verdict. Book 2’s rule still stands beside it: launch is feedback, the homepage promise is the backlog, pipes are not a business. Book 3 refuses to treat “people clicked” as that verdict.

## How to Use This Book

This is a field manual for the weeks after a loop can be finished once, not a catalog of every platform that can host a post.

**Read once for the spine.** Chapter 1 fixes the click-as-market myth. Chapters 2–3 name three pools and force a one-or-two-channel choice. Chapters 4–5 cover cold start and the pool you actually own. Chapters 6–7 treat paid acquisition as a decision instrument, with tracking and a learning period that you are not allowed to panic-kill. The Interlude is a hygiene bench: keywords, transparency tools, and the difference between selling ads on a site and buying ads for a product. Chapter 8 names failure modes. Chapter 9 packs two weeks.

**Then run Chapter 9 with the templates.** Empty theory without a dated channel card is cosplay. The templates in `templates/` are the product.

**Suggested paths**

| If you are… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Solo indie / overseas builder with a Book 2 ship verdict | Front matter → Ch 1 → Ch 3 → Ch 5 → Ch 7 → Ch 9 sprint |
| About to “be everywhere” this week | Ch 2–3 aloud → Ch 8 red lines on the wall → Ch 9 Week 1 only |
| Already spending, staring at a pretty click-through rate | Ch 1 + Ch 6–7 + tracking-acceptance template; then Ch 8 on journey leaks |
| Building a content site and thinking about AdSense | Interlude + the media-site perimeter; do not mix those metrics into a product campaign |
| Only here for Google Ads cold-start hygiene | Ch 6–7 + ads-cold-start and tracking cards; still respect the one-or-two-channel rule |

**Artifacts beat highlights.** A highlighted Kindle paragraph that never becomes a channel-card row did not count.

**Stop rules while reading:** if you catch yourself opening a fifth platform tab during Chapter 3, close it. If you catch yourself calling a click-through rate “fit,” close the ads dashboard and open the journey. Return to the primary channel and the owned hook.

### What “done” means for Book 3

You are done with Book 3’s job when you have:

1. A written primary channel, and at most one auxiliary, with a reason tied to where the job is already discussed or searched.  
2. A pool map: which actions are rented, paid, or owned, and what dies if the rented platform changes the rules.  
3. A settlement hook on every serious visit — email, account, or a reason to return that you control.  
4. Cold-start hygiene for the primary channel (the account looks like a member, not a billboard).  
5. Tracking that can name a first touch and a purchase (with value and currency) before money is spent to learn.  
6. If you buy attention: a coupon-and-budget card, a keyword intent shortlist, and a written learning-period rule that is not a mood.  
7. A stop / switch / pause / fix-the-journey rule you can execute without a pep talk.  
8. A two-week (or honest compress) verdict in writing: keep, switch, pause, or fix the journey first.

You are **not** done when you have a colorful omnichannel board, a screenshot of a healthy click-through rate, five half-warm accounts, or a slogan that says you “found PMF.”

### If you do not have a Book 2 loop

If a stranger still cannot finish the homepage promise once, at a real address, with an entitlement that lands, this book will help you buy loneliness faster. Go back to *Ship the Smallest Thing*. If you also lack a Book 1 fire-test PASS and a priced ask, go back further. Book 3 is not a workaround for a missing broom or a missing loop.

## Gatekeeper Rules (Use Before You Praise)

Every go / no-go call in this series follows the same order:

1. **Risks / holes first** — at least three, concrete.  
2. **Suggestions** — what would reduce those risks.  
3. **Conclusion** — pass / conditional pass / reject, with conditions named.

Praise before risks is how “the CTR is amazing, let’s scale” survives. Risks before conclusions is how a channel meeting earns the right to spend calendar or cash.

### Gate checklist (copy into reviews)

```text
【Risks / holes】(≥3, write first)
1. …
2. …
3. …

【Suggestions】
- …

【Conclusion】
- Pass / Conditional pass / Reject
- Conditions (if any): primary channel named, settlement hook, tracking with purchase value, coupon confirmed, learning-period rule, journey leak checked
```

If someone refuses to write risks first, they are asking you to fund a parade. Decline politely. Decline in writing.

Book 3’s motion, in one strip:

```text
Book 2 verdict (ship) + completable promise + entitlement that lands + first-touch events
        │
        ▼
A click is not a market  ←── attention ≠ job verdict; CTR is a clue, not fit
        │

        │
        ▼
One or two channels  ←── four-question gate; map is not a to-do list
        │
        ▼

        │
        ▼
Owned hook works  ←── email or account capture; recall is filtered, not blasted
        │
        ▼
Ads as a clock  ←── coupon, countries, intent words, click-start then conversion
        │
        ▼
Tracking accepts  ←── purchase + value; search-term hygiene; no blind smart bidding
        │
        ▼
Verdict: keep | switch | pause | fix the journey first
        │
        ▼
Book 4 — Earn the Click   (search systems, not a substitute for this strip)
```

**Hard boundary:** SEO systems, GEO qualification, and link-building belong in Book 4. Using them as substitutes for a primary channel and a measurable loop is how teams wait a year for a page that cannot convert, or buy a crowd for a journey that leaks.

**Handbook early priority (teaching, not prophecy):** after a survivor, a broom, and a loop, the emphasis shifts from “can a stranger finish what we sold?” to “where do we meet the next stranger, and how do we refuse to confuse their click with a market?” Do not smuggle Book 4 into Day 2 of a channel sprint.

- One-person product leads  
- Small teams that can still change direction in a week  

If you already have a staffed performance-marketing function, treat Chapters 6–8 as hygiene and skim the cold-start arguments. If you are still waiting to “get traffic” until you are on every platform, stay for the whole ride.

The desk this book was written from is an overseas-builder desk: English-first pages, Reddit and X as places people already argue about the job, email as a hook you can still use when a feed goes quiet, paid search as a clock once the loop is measurable. The psychology travels. The vendor names lean that direction on purpose. They are not endorsements, and they go stale. Official docs, current policies, and your own tests win.

## Voice Contract (English Craft)

Short sentences when the point is a gate. Concrete nouns and verbs. Case → definition → boundary. Named tools and templates over slogans.

You will not find filler that sounds like a model warming up. If a paragraph cannot survive a gatekeeper pass, it should not survive your channel week either.

**Verifiable quotes only.** When a public line appears, it is attributed. Invented testimonials are forbidden. Composite scenes use `example.invalid` and are labeled teaching sketches.

## A Note on Handbook Numbers

Wherever this manuscript mentions approximate ad spend and attributed revenue in Hong Kong dollars, a Reddit campaign with clicks and no orders, email-recall counts, follower or impression spikes, Black Friday board figures, programmatic-SEO visit narratives, AdSense path sketches, RPM ranges, daily budget ceilings, or a “wait for several distinct paying users before switching bid strategy” rule of thumb, read them as **case shapes from the Sea handbook teaching archive** — paths and warnings someone else wrote down, summarized for judgment practice. They are not your targets, not your floor, and not a promise that the same spend, click-through rate, or payout will happen twice.

Ad products, coupons, policies, keyword volumes, and community rules move. Recheck the official page and the rule of the room you are actually standing in before you spend, post, or apply.

The scarce skill is meeting the next stranger without mistaking their attention for a market. Scoreboards are optional. Parades are expensive.
