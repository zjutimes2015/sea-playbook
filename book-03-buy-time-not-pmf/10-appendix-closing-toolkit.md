# Appendix — Closing Toolkit (Worked Templates)

Blank templates live in `templates/`. Blanks are not enough on a bad Wednesday. This appendix shows **filled composites** — teaching sketches, not case-study promises, not vendor recommendations, not traffic or revenue forecasts.

Use them to see density: what “enough” looks like before you call a channel plan live.

Filled examples use invented `example.invalid` URLs and the same Thursday Packet composite as Books 1 and 2. They are **not** reports of a real company’s revenue, ranking, click-through rate, conversion, or payout.

## A. Filled Channel Card

```text
CHANNEL CARD — Thursday Packet  date: 2026-11-11

Homepage promise (one sentence):
  Get a Thursday-ready export whose client names stay readable.

Primary (this fortnight):
  Community — typography / production / design-ops threads
  where garbled client names and Thursday handoffs are already
  complained about.
  Why: one stranger already arrived from such a thread; the
  job is spoken there in the customers’ words.
  Reputation: an existing account with a history of answers.
  No hard link in a first sentence.

Auxiliary:
  Owned hook — after a successful preview, a short Thursday
  reminder they can refuse.

Later pile (dated, not whispered):
  Paid search on intent phrases — after tracking acceptance
  and after this fortnight’s membership work.
  Search systems: Book 4.
  Affiliates: after payment is dull.
  Short video: no honest five-second face yet.

Four-question gate:
  1) Room named — yes.
  2) Shortage: the next stranger, plus a way to return —
     not volume at any quality.
  3) Capital: membership exists.
  4) Catch: promise still one sentence; preview works;
     first-touch events exist; hook to be live before spend.

Risks (≥3):
  1) Adjacent art rooms are treated as the same job.
  2) Useful answers slide into a weekly advert.
  3) The coupon tab invades the later pile.

Suggestions:
  - Two useful answers before any product mention.
  - Hook on the preview screen this week.
  - Coupon tab stays behind a green tracking sheet.

Conclusion: Pass for two weeks. Reject presence as a KPI.
```

## B. Filled Pool Map and Settlement Hook

```text
POOL MAP — Thursday Packet  date: 2026-11-11

Rented: careful answers; occasional dated build notes;
        a directory pulse only in a real window.
Paid:   not this fortnight, unless the mid-gate and the
        tracking sheet both say so.
Owned:  domain mailbox (Book 2); preview-to-reminder email;
        a product hook before Thursday.

What dies if the primary room changes its rules tomorrow?
  The rented room. The list and the product hook must not.

SETTLEMENT HOOK
Moment: after a successful preview, before the pay wall.
Promise: a short reminder the evening before Thursday,
         or a note when a known failure is fixed.
         Not a weekly essay.
Refusal: one-click, visible, logged.
Recall filter (if any): previewed, did not pay, last 30 days;
         or paid, no export in 21 days. No whole-list blast.
Landing: back to the preview or the export list.

Risks (≥3):
  1) Ask arrives too early and feels like a toll.
  2) We promise a newsletter we will not write.
  3) Recall lands on the generic homepage.

Conclusion: Hook this week. Referral / affiliate later.
```

## C. Filled Ads Cold-Start and Stop Rule

```text
ADS COLD START — Thursday Packet  (prepared, not necessarily spent)

Question the spend may answer:
  Will people searching for garbled-name / client-export
  repair click, finish a preview, and will anyone pay?
  From which phrase and country?

Coupon: official entry, logged out first; progress confirmed.
Geography: only countries with a prior order.
Phrases: near garbled names, non-ASCII package names,
         Thursday handoff encoding. Not “AI design tools.”
Goal and ceiling: clicks, per-click cap, modest daily ceiling.
Switch toward purchase only after several distinct paying
users (handbook teaching shape: on the order of five —
re-check nerve and sample; do not worship the integer).

STOP RULE (written while calm)
Fortnight ceiling: [amount in local currency]
On an empty day, inspect in this order:
  tracking, phrases / rooms, landing journey — not character.
Stop means: pause / shrink / switch phrase pool / fix journey,
with a date.
Panic looks like: doubling at midnight, or zeroing because
a dashboard felt humiliating.

Risks (≥3):
  1) Coupon missed and mythologized.
  2) Trophy phrases eat the ceiling by Tuesday.
  3) Empty days become a panic-kill.

Conclusion: Conditional — only after tracking acceptance
is green and the mid-sprint gate allows a clock.
```

## E. Filled Mid-Sprint and Day-14 Verdicts (Short Forms)

```text
MID-SPRINT GATE — 2026-11-18
Primary: typography / production threads — three replies,
         one method post, one comment naming the product
         when asked.
Hook live, refusal tested: yes.
Tracking sheet: green (sandbox).
Journey leaks: pay wall still said “workspace plan”
         (repair started); one login drops a preview.
If rented reach vanished: we still have a small reminder
         list and the product hook.
Risks: (1) repairing one sentence is not yet a habit;
       (2) coupon tab still tempts an early clock;
       (3) adjacent rooms still look like “almost our job.”
Conclusion for week two: deepen primary; do not start
the clock until the leak list is empty.

DAY-14 VERDICT — 2026-11-25
Four rows: arrivals from the method post; two finished
previews; one hook; zero new payments this fortnight.
Verdict: KEEP the primary and the hook; FIX THE JOURNEY
on the remaining login drop; date a modest clock to a week
when the leak list is empty and the sheet is still green.
Conditions: no screenshot titled fit; no fifth platform;
handbook figures stay out of the verdict document.
```

## E2. Filled Four Rows (After Attention, Before a Verdict)

```text
FOUR ROWS — Thursday Packet  date: 2026-11-25
Source of attention: method post in a typography / production thread
(rented). No paid clock this fortnight.

1) Who arrived, and what were they trying to do?
   People already wrestling with broken client names in a handoff
   file, plus at least one typography-as-hobby visitor who wanted
   a font discussion, not a Thursday export.

2) What did they finish?
   Two finished previews. One reminder hook accepted. Zero new
   payments this fortnight. Original depositors unchanged.

3) What broke, in order?
   Pay-wall sentence still sounded like a workspace plan (repaired
   mid-sprint). One login path dropped a preview. The hobby visitor
   left at the first screen, which was correct — they were not
   the job.

4) What would we change if the pretty number were uglier?
   We would still repair the login drop before buying a clock.
   We would not open a fifth platform. We would not retarget the
   hobby visitor as if they had been a near-miss customer.

Note: there is no click-through rate in this document on purpose.
```

Use the four rows even when the week was quiet. Quiet is easier to misread than a spike, and easier to hide under a board.


## G. How This Appendix Relates to Book 4

If your verdict is **keep**, carry forward: the primary room, the hook, the event names (especially first touch and purchase), the later pile, and the hole list. Book 4 — *Earn the Click* — starts at search systems, answer-engine eligibility, and links as trust. It does not reopen a parade because a keyword tool felt lonely, and it does not treat a ranked page as a finished Thursday.

If your verdict is **switch**, **pause**, or **fix the journey first**, this appendix’s value is operational and emotional: you have artifacts that prove you worked like an adult. That dignity is the product of Book 3 even when the street is still quiet.

## H. Explicit Perimeter (Again)

Filled examples use invented `example.invalid` language and composite Thursday Packet scenes. They are **not** reports of a real company’s traffic, click-through rate, conversion, ranking, or payout.

Handbook figures cited elsewhere in the book — a Google Ads stretch around 2,159 / 2,823 HKD with API cost excluded, a Reddit Ads run with clicks and zero orders, a 190-to-8 recall shape, follower and impression spikes, Black Friday board figures, programmatic-SEO visit narratives, AdSense path sketches and RPM ranges, daily ceiling and “about five purchasers” teaching parameters — remain teaching cases only.

No ticked card guarantees attention, orders, or income, and no composite fortnight should be quoted as if it happened to a named company. A complete toolkit only raises the odds you will refuse a parade, refuse a screenshot ceremony, and refuse to borrow a life from a rented room, before any of those becomes your week.
