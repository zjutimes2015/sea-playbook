# Chapter 3 — Own One or Two Channels

The Thursday Packet builder presented the omnichannel board as if it were seriousness.

Five platforms had owners, which is to say the same person had written their own name five times. Each platform had a “content pillar.” None of the pillars mentioned garbled client names, Thursday handoffs, or the one comment thread that had already sent a stranger to the site. A column on the right said **KPI: presence**.

Presence is not a KPI. It is a mood.

The person who had arrived from the typography-and-exports thread did not need presence. They needed a room where that complaint was already allowed to be specific, and a product that finished the job. Everything else on the board was a way to avoid choosing.

This chapter is the cut. The channel map in the handbook is a map. It is not a chore list. You will leave with one primary channel, at most one auxiliary, and a written reason that can survive a gatekeeper who is not impressed by logo rows.

## What the Map Is For (and What It Is Not)

A useful map for an overseas indie site, rewritten in this book’s language, looks like this:

| Family | Typical motion | Clock | When it fits | Where this series puts the detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Search assets** | Intent pages, a content system | Slow, then long | After the queries are real | Book 4; this book only locates the family |
| **Paid acquisition** | Search or social ads; brand-term insurance | Fast | When the journey is measurable | Chapters 6–7 |
| **Community** | Membership, useful posts, patient comments | Medium | Cold start, demand checks, support for search | Chapter 4 |
| **Short social** | X or similar: a human account, then a product | Medium | A face and a niche, not a dump of changelogs | Chapter 4 |
| **Video search** | Tutorials, short demos, occasional timely pieces | Medium to long | When the job can be seen in seconds, or taught | Chapter 4 |
| **Build in public** | Dated process, not a diary of moods | Medium | Early trust, if you can tell the truth | Chapter 4 |
| **Directories and seasonal pulses** | Launch lists, promo calendars | A pulse | A window, not a personality | Chapter 8’s window warning |
| **Referrals and affiliates** | Users bring users; later, a program | Medium to long | After payment is steady and the product deserves it | Chapter 5 |
| **Email and other owned reach** | Lifecycle, recall, unpaid-but-warm follow-up | Compounding | As soon as you have a hook | Chapter 5 |
| **Opportunistic windows** | Riding a new query; defending a brand term | Short | Only with a page that can catch the visit | Chapter 8; Ads detail in Chapter 6 |

The map’s job is to help you **see families**, not to help you collect stamps.

**Risks**

1. Copying someone else’s spike into your chore list.  
2. Splitting one tired person across five families, then calling the exhaustion “hustle.”  
3. Starting an affiliate program before anyone will pay, or starting paid acquisition before anyone can finish the promise.

**Suggestion — a four-question gate, in this order:**

1. **Where do people already discuss or search this job?** Tool-heavy complaints often live in Reddit, X, or search. A visual five-second job may belong on YouTube. A budget season may belong to a pulse. If you cannot name the room or the query, you are not choosing a channel. You are decorating a board.  
2. **What are you short of right now — a test, volume, or a way to return?** Tests lean community and build-in-public. Volume, once the path works, may lean ads or later search. Return lean email and product hooks. Mixing these shortages is how a team buys volume to avoid a test, or writes a newsletter to avoid picking a room.  
3. **Do you have the reputation capital this room requires?** A new account that hard-sells is not a channel plan. It is a donation to the spam filter.  
4. **Can you catch and measure the visit?** If the landing sentence, the price, the events, and the hook are not ready, a new channel only amplifies confusion. The conversion album in the handbook repeats this until it is boring. Boring is the point.

**Conclusion.** Choose from the scene and the stage you are in. Combine with a primary, an auxiliary, and a settlement hook. Do not use the map as a dare.

## Combination Rules That Survive a Tuesday

The handbook compresses combination into four habits. I will keep them as habits, not as commandments from a mountain.

- **Primary and auxiliary, named.** At most one primary and one helper in the same fortnight — for example, Reddit as the room, email as the hook. A helper that is secretly a second primary will eat the fortnight.  
- **One fish, several meals — only after the fish exists.** A YouTube piece can be cut for short social. A promo page can serve search, a directory, and a modest campaign. This is reuse, not a reason to start three families on Monday because reuse sounds efficient.  
- **Rent or buy, then settle.** Every rented or paid action must answer: *can this visit leave an email, an account, or a reason to return that we control?* If the answer is no, you are entertaining the platform, not building a pool.  
- **Pulses amplify; they do not replace Tuesdays.** A Black Friday combination — a strong page, documents that can sell without a call, early directory submissions, a careful community mention — is a teaching structure in the handbook, sometimes told with a week-board figure attached. Copy the structure if the product already stands. Do not copy the figure. Do not live inside a pulse because ordinary Tuesdays feel unglamorous.

Thursday Packet, on an ordinary Tuesday, did not need a pulse. It needed a primary room where broken exports were already a complaint, and a hook for the people who previewed a repair and left.

## A Worked Channel Card (Composite)

```text
CHANNEL CARD — Thursday Packet  date: 2026-11-11

Primary (this fortnight):
  Community — typography / production / design-ops threads
  where people already complain about broken client names
  and Thursday handoffs.
  Why: one stranger already arrived from such a thread;
  the job is spoken there in the customers’ words.
  Reputation capital: existing account with a history of
  answers; no hard link in the first sentence.

Auxiliary (this fortnight):
  Owned hook — after a successful preview, offer a short
  “Thursday reminder” email they can refuse.
  Why: the primary is rented; the hook is what we keep.

Explicitly later (written, not whispered):
  Paid search on intent phrases, after tracking acceptance
  and after this fortnight’s membership work.
  Search assets / SEO systems: Book 4, not this board.
  Affiliate program: after payment is boringly steady.
  Short video: no face and no five-second demo yet; do not
  fake one.

Four-question gate:
  1) Room named above — yes.
  2) Shortage: discovery of the next stranger, plus a way
     to return — not “volume at any quality.”
  3) Capital: membership exists; if it did not, the first
     work would be membership, not a link.
  4) Catch: homepage promise still one sentence; preview
     works; first-touch events exist; email hook to be
     wired this week before any spend.

Risks (≥3):
  1) Treating related art communities as the same job
     (see the unsent-letter click trap in Chapter 1).
  2) Turning useful answers into a weekly advert.
  3) Letting “later: paid search” sneak into this week
     because the coupon tab is open.

Suggestions:
  - Write two useful answers before any product mention.
  - Wire the hook on the preview screen.
  - Put the coupon tab behind tracking acceptance.

Conclusion: Pass for a two-week primary + auxiliary.
Reject omnichannel presence as a KPI.
```

If your card cannot name a later pile, the later pile will invade the fortnight. Forbidden scope was a living document in Book 2. Forbidden channels should be one here.

## Programmatic SEO, Briefly, So It Does Not Hijack the Fortnight

The handbook records a dramatic programmatic-SEO narrative — including a “millions of visits” shape — and then sends the engineering to the SEO volume. I will do the same, in one paragraph, so the drama cannot masquerade as a cold-start plan.

Search assets can become a long-cycle primary *family* once queries are real, templates have quality gates, and thin pages are refused. That family is not a substitute for a room you can enter this month. It is not a social strategy. If you are tempted to “just generate the pages” because Chapter 3 asked you to choose only one or two channels, you are trying to escape a choice by starting a factory. Book 4 is where that factory will be inspected. It will not be kinder than this paragraph.

## Where Choosing Falsifies Itself

One-or-two-channel discipline can rot.

It fails when the primary is chosen for the builder’s comfort rather than the job’s habitat. A designer who likes short video will invent a reason to dance about file encoding. A former SEO contractor will invent a reason to write thirty pages before speaking to anyone. Comfort is not a gate.

It fails when “one channel” becomes a superstition that forbids a legitimate helper — especially an owned hook. The rule is one primary and at most one auxiliary, not one action for the rest of your life.

It fails when the team picks a room, gets one cold week, and flees to a new logo. Channels have learning periods too. Chapter 7 will say this about ads. It is also true of a community you just started treating like a member.

It fails when the card is used as a moral license to ignore a leak. A well-chosen room that sends people into a broken first screen is still a bad fortnight. Fix the journey, or you are choosing a channel as costume.

## FAQ (Choosing Without a Parade)

**Can the auxiliary be paid ads while the primary is a community?**  
Yes, if tracking accepts and the ads exist to answer a written question, not to soothe quiet. In many first fortnights, the better auxiliary is the owned hook. Ads can be the next primary once the room and the hook are real.

**What if our buyers are on two continents and two platforms?**  
You still need a primary *this fortnight*. A later card can change the primary. Two primaries on day one is how both stay shallow.

**What if a partner demands a presence plan?**  
Give them the card, the later pile, and the death sentence from Chapter 2. If they still want five logos, they are asking for a drawing. Decide whether that drawing is worth a political week. Do not confuse it with work.

## When the Primary Should Change (and When It Should Not)

A primary is a fortnight’s bet, not a marriage. It should change when you can write a reason that is about the job’s habitat, not about your boredom.

**Change it when:**

- The room does not actually speak this job — adjacent mood, different broom — and you have given membership a fair try, not a single quiet Tuesday.  
- A different family now matches the shortage: the test is done, the hook works, and a modest search clock is the honest next primary.  
- Policy or reputation weather makes the room unusable for a dated stretch (a ban, a rule change, a risk you will not take). Write the date you will look again.

**Do not change it when:**

- You are simply tired of being a member. Membership is tiring. That is part of the bill.  
- A pulse looked more exciting than a Tuesday.  
- A partner asked for a logo row.  
- One post did not travel. One post is weather.

When you switch, retire the old primary into the later pile or into an auxiliary, on purpose. Do not leave five “soft primaries” running because you felt guilty abandoning a board. Guilt is not a channel mix.

Thursday Packet might, after two honest fortnights in typography threads and a green tracking sheet, make intent search the new primary and keep the room as the auxiliary. That is a change of shortage: from “can we meet the next stranger as members?” to “can we buy time among people already querying the job?” It is not a betrayal of community. It is a new card.

## Takeaway

**Rule:** The channel map is for choosing and combining, not for collecting stamps. Own one primary and at most one auxiliary at a time.

**This week:** Fill a channel card. Name the later pile in public (or at least in a dated file). Wire the settlement hook before you add a logo.

**Not a promise:** Seasonal board figures and programmatic-SEO visit narratives in the handbook are shapes for structure, not a reason to skip the cut.
