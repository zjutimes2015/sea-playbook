## 完整章节大纲（约 30k 词）

### Front matter
- **Promise：** 读完你能用「抱怨账本」在 7 天内否决 80% 的伪需求，并对留下来的那条跑一次预售。
- **Not a promise：** 不保证 $10k MRR；个案只作路径说明。

### Ch 1 · The Inspiration Wall（开篇场景）
会议室「灵感池」便利贴 vs 差评夹。点题：找需求 ≠ 找灵感。  
带走物：一句话纪律——*Inspiration is a seed; a complaint is evidence.*

### Ch 2 · Why Organizations Prefer Ideas
激励错位：体面、可交差、像产出。Blank：「大楼里没有事实」。  
带走物：把「本周产出」从功能点改成「结构化抱怨条数 + 被否决假设数」。

### Ch 3 · The Complaint Signal（三件套）
抱怨同时满足：已在用、有频率、有缺口。  
反例：meme、单条吐槽、熟人「挺好的」。  
带走物：抱怨入账四问模板。

### Ch 4 · The Fire Test（刚需）
破扫帚 vs 精致灭火器；Must-have vs Nice-to-have。  
四种刚需形态：高频痛 / 规模化灾难 / 脏活 / 已有人付费。  
带走物：一页灭火测试清单；不过关禁止重开发。

### Ch 5 · Jobs, Not Personas
奶昔研究逻辑（公开可核）；任务说不清，需求未立。  
带走物：JTBD 一句话句式（When… I want to… so I can…）。

### Ch 6 · Four Sources, One Chain
痛点 / 市场行为 / 竞品差评 / 技术窗口——串成链路，不单点迷信。  
含 Reddit / 插件差评 / Fiverr 已付服务 / GitHub 对标。  
带走物：机会卡字段表（信号源、重复度、付费线索、开源差距、风险）。

### Ch 7 · Sell Air（验证先于开发）
预售不是骗局：说清做什么、怎么解决、真实价格。  
点赞≠付费；预售没人买 = 省时间。  
带走物：7 日验证剧本（落地页 + 支付链 + 1–2 个流量源）。

### Ch 8 · Where the Method Breaks（自我证伪）
单源情绪、抱怨→功能清单、跳过开源对标、永远再挖一周、自动化越界。  
带走物：五条「停用本方法」红线。

### Ch 9 · The One-Week Complaint Sprint（操作篇）
Day1 选垂直社区 → Day2–3 结构化抓取 → Day4 灭火+JTBD → Day5 交叉验证 → Day6–7 卖空气。  
带走物：可打印周计划 + 机会库空表。

### Ch 10 · After the Gate（简短收束）
过闸后才谈产品四维、定价、转化；回到开场白板：灵感池清空，抱怨账本留下。  
作者注：这是系列第一本；Ads / SEO 等可续写，但不在本书范围。

- 模板附录（四问 / 灭火 / JTBD / 机会卡 / 预售文案骨架）
- 延伸阅读：Blank *Startup Owner's Manual*；Christensen JTBD 公开材料；Sea 手册找需求册

## 第 1 章开篇（英文稿 · 可直接往下写）

# Chapter 1 — The Inspiration Wall

The whiteboard’s top-right column said **Inspiration Pool**.

Yellow sticky notes covered half the wall. Someone had written “AI journal app.” Someone else: “Clone X, add three differentiators.” A third: “The market *must* need this.” The product lead was already estimating weeks. Engineering was counting endpoints. The founder’s finger hovered over the word **Approve** for three long seconds.

In those three seconds, the room was arguing about the wrong thing.

They were asking whether a brain-invented idea should count as a demand that already existed.

Get that wrong, and the next few months of code become polite spinning.

I have sat in that room—sometimes as the person with the sticky note, sometimes as the person who should have said no. The sticky notes feel productive. They photograph well. They fit a slide titled “Innovation.” A folder of one-star reviews does not. Neither does a Reddit thread where strangers call your favorite workflow “hell.”

So teams optimize for the photogenic version of discovery.

This book is about the other version.

**Finding demand is not finding inspiration.** Ideas are cheap. What is scarce is the judgment to stand at the source of real pain—and the discipline to treat that pain as a hypothesis until money moves.

A complaint is often three signals glued together:

1. The person is already using *something* (the scene is real).  
2. They hit the problem on a schedule (frequency, not a one-off mood).  
3. The current fix still fails (a gap you might close cheaper).

An inspiration sticky usually has none of those. It has vibes.

Steve Blank’s old line still earns its keep: *There are no facts inside your building, so get outside.* The brainstorm is opinion with better lighting. Repeated complaints, bad reviews, and “is there a tool for this?” posts are the raw ore of facts. Ore is not yet a product. But it is closer to evidence than a wall of yellow paper.

You will not finish this chapter with a billion-dollar idea. You will finish with a rule you can say out loud in the next meeting:

> Inspiration is a seed.  
> A complaint is evidence.  
> A payment is a verdict.

Everything else in this book—the fire test, the job-to-be-done sentence, the four-source chain, selling air before you write code—is just how to keep that rule from rotting into another slogan.

Clear the Inspiration Pool for one week. Open a Complaint Ledger instead. If the ledger stays empty, you do not have a creativity problem. You have a listening problem—or a courage problem about what you might hear.

Either way, the sticky notes can wait.

## 中文对照开篇（若先写中文版）

白板右上角那一栏，写着三个大字：**灵感池**。

黄色便利贴贴满了半面墙。有人写「做个 AI 日记」，有人写「对标某某再加三点差异」，有人写「市场一定需要」。产品在估工期，技术在估接口，老板的手指在「立项」上停了三秒。

那三秒里，房间争错了题目：我们是不是要把脑内发明，当成已经立住的需求。

争错了，后面几个月代码都会变成礼貌的空转。

**找需求不是找灵感。** 点子不稀缺；稀缺的是站在真实痛苦源头时的判断力，以及在钱移动之前，把痛苦只当假设的纪律。

灵感是种子。抱怨是证据。付款是裁决。
