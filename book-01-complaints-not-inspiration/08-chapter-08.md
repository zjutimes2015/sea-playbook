# Chapter 8 — Where Demand Finding Breaks

Every useful method dies in characteristic ways. If you do not name those ways, the method becomes a costume for the same old inspiration—only now with spreadsheets.

This chapter is self-falsification on purpose. Complaint ledgers, fire tests, job sentences, four-source chains, and pre-sales can each rot. Rotten tools still look like tools. That is why they are dangerous.

Read this as red lines: conditions under which you should **stop using this method as currently practiced**—not as poetic humility at the end of a blog post.

---

## Failure Mode 1 — Single-Source Emotion

**What it looks like:** one subreddit mood, one viral dunk, one Discord meltdown, one influencer rant. The ledger fills with screenshots from a single weather system.

**Why it breaks:** emotion is contagious; contagion is not frequency; frequency is not payment. You will overfit to people who enjoy posting.

**Brake:** require independent repeats across time or platform before `theme` becomes `fire_test_next`. If you cannot find a second pond, write `MORE SIGNAL`—or kill.

**Stop using the method when:** you catch yourself saying “the internet is angry about X” as if that were a card.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Complaint → Feature Bingo

**What it looks like:** every insult becomes a ticket. Roadmaps become museums (Chapter 5). “We listen” becomes a synonym for “we cannot choose.”

**Why it breaks:** complaints are inputs to jobs and fires, not a shopping list. Bingo creates products with no hireable progress.

**Brake:** no feature earns a ticket until it rhymes with a JTBD one-liner and a PASS fire page. Park extras in a souvenir lot.

**Stop using the method when:** your “complaint-driven roadmap” is longer than your forbidden_scope list.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Skipping Open-Source (and Other Honesty Fields)

**What it looks like:** `open_source_gap: not checked` for weeks; “ours will be cleaner”; rebuilding libraries with better CSS; ignoring a marketplace that already sells the broom as a service.

**Why it breaks:** you spend your scarce months on undifferentiated rebuilds. Demand may be real and still not worth *your* build.

**Brake:** no `ready_for_presale` while open-source (or “buy the service”) is unchecked. Write why you still deserve to exist in one uncomfortable paragraph.

**Stop using the method when:** honesty fields become optional decorations.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Eternal Another Week of Digging

**What it looks like:** MORE SIGNAL forever; research cosplay; dashboards of pain; no kills; no priced page.

**Why it breaks:** listening theater (Chapter 2) returns wearing a clipboard. You feel rigorous while avoiding verdicts.

**Brake:** timers. Two weeks max on MORE SIGNAL without either a kill, a PASS+presale, or a written reason to extend once. Count kills as output.

**Stop using the method when:** “we’re still validating” is your personality.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Automation Past the Boundary

**What it looks like:** auto-commenting, auto-DMing, auto-posting “helpful” prompts into communities; bots that turn ore veins into spam; scrapers that violate terms you pretend not to see.

**Why it breaks:** you poison the well, get banned, and teach your team that demand work equals acquisition abuse. Also: automated judgment is usually automated bias.

**Brake:** automation may listen, clean, tag, and draft. Humans send, judge, and kill. Period.

**Stop using the method when:** your “research stack” requires hiding your identity from the communities you mine.

---

## Failure Mode 6 — Fire Test as Vibes

**What it looks like:** PASS without risks; PASS without a grab-today substitute; everything is must-have because you like it; marketing will “create the fire.”

**Why it breaks:** Chapter 4 becomes a rubber stamp. Nice-to-have wears a broom costume.

**Brake:** risks first; ugly-broom acceptance question mandatory; quota of FAILs.

**Stop using the method when:** PASS rate approaches 100%.

---

## Failure Mode 7 — JTBD as Incense

**What it looks like:** acronyms without sentences; sticky-note slogans; “AI” in every want-clause; personas renamed as jobs.

**Why it breaks:** you get the prestige of theory without the rudeness of specificity.

**Brake:** when / want / so I can on a full page; buzzword removal stress test; competing hires listed.

**Stop using the method when:** nobody can point to the current battlefield job without opening a slide.

---

## Failure Mode 8 — Pre-Sales as Theater or Slime

**What it looks like:** waitlists without prices; fake countdown ethics; friend-only receipts; charging for what you cannot describe; extending “seven days” into a quarter to avoid writing a kill.

**Why it breaks:** you either learn nothing or learn how to disrespect buyers. Both destroy the verdict.

**Brake:** real number, stranger path, delivery story, refund clarity, hard stop date.

**Stop using the method when:** you need hype language to feel OK about the page.

---

## Failure Mode 9 — Inner-Loop Products and Wrong Competitors

**What it looks like:** endless tools for other indie hackers; hard wars against hundred-person teams with ad firehoses; “OS for everything” wedges; boring industry dirty work ignored because it is unfashionable.

**Why it breaks:** the chain can look correct while your life becomes impossible. Method success ≠ life fit.

**Brake:** prefer niches beside real workflows with wallets; write competitor realism in risks; allow “real but not for us” FAILs.

**Stop using the method when:** every card targets people exactly like your Twitter mutuals.

---

## Failure Mode 10 — Turning the Perimeter into Fine Print

**What it looks like:** handbook cases (~**$10k MRR** repair stories, ~**8** backlinks / ~**41K** visits shapes) quietly become OKRs; “no promises” in the preface, prophecy in the standup.

**Why it breaks:** you reintroduce inspiration as numeric destiny. The method’s honesty dies first; the calendar dies second.

**Brake:** say “case, not promise” out loud when numbers appear. Ban copying case numbers into goals.

**Stop using the method when:** you catch yourself selling certainty to yourself.

---

## Five Red Lines (Print Them)

1. **No single-source PASS.**  
2. **No feature tickets without a job rhyme.**  
3. **No ready_for_presale with open-source unchecked.**  
4. **No automation that speaks to communities as if it were you without you.**  
5. **No seven-day script without a dated verdict—including kill.**  

If you break a red line, you do not need a workshop. You need a stop. Repair the practice, then continue. Continuing while broken is how methodologies become cult kitsch.

## A Postmortem Template (When a Bet Dies)

```text
POSTMORTEM — theme: ____  date: ____
What we believed:
What evidence we had:
Which failure mode bit us (1–10):
What we skipped (ledger / fire / JTBD / chain / air):
What we will refuse next time:
Kill sentence for the public list:
```

Postmortems without blame theater beat silent disappearances. Silent disappearances teach nothing and keep the haunted roadmap alive.

## What Still Works When Parts Break

Even if you temporarily stop “the full method,” you can keep smaller hygiene: write risks first; prefer stranger words; treat payment as stronger than applause; kill in public. Hygiene is the portable core. Frameworks are scaffolding. Do not burn the core because a scaffold warped.

## Light Meta-Falsification

This chapter can itself become performative—listing failure modes as virtue without changing behavior. If you highlight these lines and still run feature bingo next week, you did not read; you decorated.

Pick one failure mode you personally commit. Put it on a sticky where you cannot help seeing it. That sticky is allowed. Inspiration stickies still are not.

## How Rot Shows Up in Standups

Listen for these phrases. They are smoke alarms:

- “Everyone’s talking about it.” (single-source / weather)  
- “We’ll just add it to the backlog.” (feature bingo)  
- “Open source isn’t polished enough.” (unchecked honesty wearing taste)  
- “Give us one more week of research.” (eternal digging)  
- “The bot can engage for us.” (automation boundary breach)  
- “It’s obviously must-have.” (vibe PASS)  
- “Our JTBD is innovation.” (incense)  
- “The waitlist is exploding.” (unpriced theater)  
- “Our users are builders like us.” (inner loop)  
- “This could be a $10k MRR thing like that case.” (perimeter collapse)

When you hear one, you do not need a moral speech. Ask for the artifact: second source, job sentence, open-source note, kill date, checkout link, risk block. Artifacts beat arguments.

### Team size note

Solo builders rot too—usually via eternal digging and vibe PASSes, because nobody is there to be rude. If you are solo, schedule a weekly “stranger review”: send one fire page or one landing draft to someone who does not share your upside. Pay them in cash or favor. Friendship-only review recreates friend-money bias.

## Recovery Plan After You Catch Yourself

1. **Stop the build** that depended on the rotten step.  
2. **Write the failure mode number** in the card/ledger.  
3. **Restore the missing artifact** or kill.  
4. **Add one process scar:** a checklist item, a timer, a red line on the wall.  
5. **Only then** resume.

Skipping to step 5 is how smart teams stay stuck. Shame is optional. Sequence is not.

---

## When the Market Moves Faster Than Your Ledger

Sometimes a real window is short: a platform change, a policy shift, a model capability. Speed matters—but speed without red lines is just panic with better tools. Compress the chain into the thirty-minute pass (Chapter 6), run a shorter priced test, and accept that some windows close. Closed windows are not personal insults. They are weather. Do not use “speed” as a reason to delete open-source checks or refund clarity.

## A Quarterly Method Audit (Fifteen Minutes)

Once a quarter, skim your last ten cards and ask:

- How many single-source PASSes slipped through?  
- How many features shipped without job rhyme?  
- How often was open-source “not checked”?  
- How many MORE SIGNALs aged past two weeks?  
- Did any bot speak for us?  
- What was our FAIL rate on fire tests?  
- Did any case number become a goal?

Score lightly. Pick one scar to fix. Do not create a bureaucracy that replaces shipping verdicts. The audit exists to keep Chapter 8 from becoming unread scripture.




## Case Study Shape: A Method That Ate Itself (Composite)

A small team ran ledgers faithfully for a month. Row count looked impressive. They declared themselves “complaint-driven.” Then the rot arrived in a bundle: they auto-drafted replies in a forum; they PASSed three fires in one afternoon without risks; they shipped a changelog of bingo features; they cited a handbook-like revenue case in a Friday all-hands as if it were a forecast. Two months later they had a quieter chart and a louder story about “market timing.”

What would have saved them was not more ideology. It was red lines with teeth: no bot speech; one PASS per week maximum until FAILs appeared; feature freeze until job rhyme; ban on pasting case numbers into goals; a dated pre-sale that could kill the favorite theme. The method did not fail as math. It failed as discipline.

If this composite feels uncomfortably close, treat that feeling as a gift. Closeness means you can intervene before the quiet chart.

### The “we’re different” exemption

Everyone’s favorite escape hatch is uniqueness: our market is different; our founders are technical enough to skip air; our community hates selling; our enterprise buyers never pay early. Sometimes constraints are real. Write them as risks and redesign the verdict surface (paid pilot, SOW, deposit). Do not write them as a blanket exemption from Chapter 8. Uniqueness is usually ego’s preferred citation format.

## Teaching Juniors Without Creating Zealots

If you bring juniors into complaint work, teach artifacts before vocabulary. A filled fire page beats a lecture on Christensen. A kill sentence beats a quiz on Blank. Zealots memorize words and bully teammates with acronyms. Practitioners produce dated decisions.

Pair juniors with a red-line buddy for one sprint. The buddy’s job is not to be clever. It is to point at missing fields. Missing fields are how juniors learn; humiliation is how they learn to hide.

### Personal inventory (do this once after reading)

Circle the two modes you have committed in the last year. Write one sentence each about the cost. Put the sentences in your sprint Day 0 folder. People improve methods by attaching costs to scars—not by collecting frameworks like stamps.

## When Leadership Wants Speed Theater

Leaders under pressure may ask you to “just ship” past red lines. Your options are clearer than they feel:

1. Produce a one-page fire+risk note that makes the skip visible.  
2. Offer a seven-day air test as the fast path—often faster than a fake build.  
3. If overruled, write the skip into the decision log so learning survives.  

You may not win the argument. You can still prevent amnesia. Amnesia is how organizations repeat polite spinning and call it culture.



### Last line before the takeaway

If Book 1 becomes a personality—someone who only kills, never ships—you have overcorrected into a new inspiration: the romance of skepticism. Skepticism is a tool. Verdicts sometimes say proceed. Proceeding without romance is still allowed.

When in doubt, prefer a smaller claim and a faster kill over a grander method and a slower self-deception. Grand methods flatter. Small claims compound.
