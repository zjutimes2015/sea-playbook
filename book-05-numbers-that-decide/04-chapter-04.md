# Chapter 4 — Find the Breakpoint Before You Fire

Once the events were accepted, the Thursday Packet room acquired a new talent: panic with evidence.

A week of mixed visits arrived — some from the typography-and-exports room, some from the `/repair` query, a few from a leftover ads test. The weekly total of sessions looked less lonely than the month before. The weekly total of payments did not. A hand went to the two familiar levers: raise the ads clock, and rewrite the homepage headline.

Both levers can be right, later. Both are usually wrong when you have not named the step that broke.

This chapter is a repair order. A funnel is not a trophy wall, and it is not a growth guarantee. It is a way to decide which wound to touch first. Retention sits beside it, read in groups, not as a single frightening percentage. If you add fuel to a path that drops people before they see the job, you are not being aggressive. You are being expensive.

---

## A Minimum Path You Can Say Out Loud

You may define your steps differently than a neighbor does. You may not change the definition mid-week so that a slide looks kinder. Write the steps, then keep them still long enough to learn.

A minimum path for an overseas product that takes money, and the one Thursday Packet used, looks like this:

1. **Arrive** on the landing or tool page that claims the job.  
2. **Start** the serious action (upload, sign-up, or the main call).  
3. **Finish sign-up**, if an account is required. If it is not required, say so, and do not pretend a missing step is a triumph.  
4. **First core value** — preview rendered, export done, analysis finished.  
5. **Payment clicked.**  
6. **Payment succeeded**, with a transaction id.

Thursday Packet’s early chart, after acceptance, did not look like a cliff at payment. It looked like a slide between preview and export. People saw repaired names. Fewer people took the Thursday-ready file. Fewer still reached checkout. The ads lever and the homepage lever would have touched the wrong end of the path.

### Use the steepest slope first

Each week, look for the step where the slope is steepest. Fix that step, or test one change on that step. Do not “improve the funnel” by touching five surfaces at once. If you do, Chapter 7 will have nothing honest to attribute.

A useful companion question sits beside the slope: how do people who completed core value behave later, compared with people who did not? If the ones who exported come back and the ones who only previewed do not, you have an activation problem, not yet a media problem. If even the exporters vanish and never pay, you have a value or a pricing problem. If exporters pay and do not return, you may have a completed-job product, which is allowed, and you should not scold it with a SaaS retention hymn.

Qualitative check, always: drop the breakpoint page into recordings and maps (Chapter 6) before you blame the visitor.

## How Thursday Packet Found the Break (Composite)

The first accepted week was small. Small is not a reason to invent a story, and it is not a reason to ignore a pattern that recordings can confirm.

On `/repair`, most people who arrived from the typography-and-exports hook reached the preview. The preview was the product’s pride: names that had been dirty were clean, and they were still the client’s names, not a sample. The export control sat under a typographic specimen, styled as a caption. Desktop users who already knew the product found it. New visitors, especially on a narrower screen, treated the caption as decoration.

The steepest slope was there: preview ready, export not done.

The first repair was not a new brand film. It was a button that looked like a button, a sentence that said “Download the Thursday-ready file,” and a recording pass to see whether the pointer still wandered. Payment copy was left alone for a week. The ads clock was left alone for a week. Homepage poetry was left alone for a week. That restraint is the method. It will feel slow. It is faster than a month of mixed changes and a retrospective that can only shrug.

Did export-complete rise after the button? In a composite, we can say the slope eased and that a later week was allowed to look at checkout. We cannot say, and this book will not say, that your similar button will raise revenue by a named percent. The funnel is a repair order. The size of the gift is a later experiment.

## Retention Without the Panic Percentage

Retention reports are where calm people become superstitious. A day-two number wobbles, a seasonal campaign ends, a typography group goes quiet for a holiday week, and the room talks about product-market collapse.

Read retention in groups, or do not read it yet.

Fix a cohort in writing: new users from the same week, from the same channel if you can, split by whether they completed the core event. Then ask a better question than “is day-two good?” Ask: after which action are people more willing to return? That question designs activation. It does not design a promised curve of monthly active users.

If the sample is tiny, the wobble is noise. Improve event quality and wait for a larger group before you start a “retention campaign.” A campaign that shouts at ten people is not discipline. It is theater.

If retention is weak among people who never reached core value, adding traffic is usually waste. Fix whether the value was received. If retention is weak among people who did receive it, you have a harder, more honest product question: was the job a one-time repair, and should you price it that way, or did you promise a habit you do not support?

Thursday Packet, truthfully, leaned toward a weekly job, not a daily habit. A typography-and-exports room thinks in Thursdays. A retention hymn copied from a social app would have scolded the product for being what it was. The useful split remained: people who had exported once were the people who might export next Thursday. People who had only hovered were not a retention problem. They were an activation problem, or they were wanderers.

## What You May Not Do With a Better Step

A cleaner step can precede more income. It can also precede a quieter chart that is simply more honest. This book will not convert a step-rate improvement into a growth promise, and you should not either, not in a slide, not in a store listing, not in a conversation with a tired partner.

You also may not change the funnel definition to flatter the week. If you start counting from “preview ready” because “arrive” looks bleak, write a new funnel with a new name and a new start date. Do not silently shorten the old one.

You may not, when the break is still on `/repair`, “fix it” by buying more of the same visits. That is the 1,300-visit, zero-order shape from the source archive, reduced to a habit: observation high, decision layer empty, fuel added anyway. The number 1,300 is not your forecast. The refusal is the lesson.

## Two Funnels, One Blender (Again)

If paid visits and typography-room visits share a single funnel, you will “repair” a blend. Book 3’s leftover ads test and Book 4’s generic-converter search are not the same entrance as a hook that already knows the Thursday job. You may draw one minimum path for the product. You should still read the slope inside each serious source, or you will raise a headline that the typography room never needed and the search visitor never understood.

A second blender is definitional. One teammate starts the path at “arrived on the marketing home.” Another starts it at “preview ready,” because the home looks bleak this week. Both can be honest views. They cannot be the same named funnel. If you need a shorter view, give it a new name and a start date. Silent shortening is how a repair order becomes a compliment.

“Improve the funnel” is a slogan, cousin to “improve the brand.” The legal sentence names a step, a change, and a thing you will not touch. If you cannot name those, you are not yet in Chapter 4. You are in the indoor plan from Chapter 1.

## Where This Framing Falsifies Itself

**A one-step product has a one-step funnel.** If the entire job is a single click that also charges, your “steepest slope” talk can become theater. Watch recordings, watch refunds, and skip the six-box diagram.

**Sometimes the break really is at the top of the path**, and the correct next move really is more qualified visits, or a Book 4 page that can be found. The test is whether people who already arrive can finish. If they can, and they simply do not arrive, this chapter will not tell you to polish a healthy button forever.

**A marketplace or a multi-sided tool may need two funnels.** Write both. Do not average them into a single “conversion rate” and then pick a lever at random.

**Huge samples can make small slopes look urgent.** Statistical confidence is not the same as practical importance. A 0.4-point dip on a huge site may be real as arithmetic and still not be this week’s job. Name the cost of the change before you treat every slope as a fire.

---

## Takeaway

**Rule:** A funnel is a repair order. The steepest honest slope goes first, and a better step is not a revenue promise.

**This week:** Write your steps, keep the definitions still, mark the steepest break, and watch that page in recordings before you touch ads, headlines, or five surfaces at once.
