<p><b>A dashboard is not a decision. An average is not a customer. A story is not a retrospective.</b></p>

<p><i>Numbers That Decide</i> is Book 5 of <b>The Sea Playbook</b>—a practical English field manual for indie developers, overseas builders, and small product teams who already have a page that can finish a job and are about to confuse a moving chart with a living product.</p>

<p>If your “analytics plan” still looks like a blessing screenshot, a blended conversion rate, or a weekly memoir, this book replaces dashboard theater with a portable discipline:</p>

<ul>
<li>Treat <b>counts, completed jobs, comparisons, and verdicts</b> as four different events</li>
<li>Instrument on the launch day: Search Console, analytics, recordings, defended events, and the ledger when money moves</li>
<li>Write an <b>event contract</b> you can accept—core value, path, and intent—not a pile of decorative clicks</li>
<li>Use a funnel as a <b>repair order</b>: find the steepest break before you add fuel</li>
<li>Kill the <b>average user</b>; split channel, device, and “did the job or not”</li>
<li>Treat recordings as a <b>diagnosis</b>, and an assistant’s summary as a first pass you must check</li>
<li>Write attribution as a <b>rule</b> (bids, care, and money), not as physics</li>
<li>Run a retrospective with <b>five columns and three prior lines</b>, ending in one verdict: stop, change, or scale</li>
<li>Use a <b>one-person veto</b> on fatal habits, energy, compliance, and vanity stories</li>
</ul>

<p>You get templates, worked composites, gatekeeper review format (risks before conclusions), an interlude on solo-company vetoes, and failure modes that show where decision rituals break.</p>

<p><b>What this book does not promise:</b> no guaranteed growth, conversion, retention, correct attribution, or revenue tier. Handbook teaching cases mentioned inside (including timing and income shapes from a source archive) are illustrations for judgment practice—not forecasts for your site.</p>

<p>Best read after Book 4: <i>Earn the Click</i>. This is the fifth and last book in the planned path.</p>
